# Five Windows

A little house shared by five friends who live far apart: Rithika in Canberra, Neha in New Jersey, Amirdhavarshini in Tamil Nadu, and Rithanya and Aswathy in Bangalore. Everyone has a room, and everyone shares the living room, the kitchen, the garden and Shinzo the dog.

You come in whenever you're free, leave something for someone, and go. Whoever comes by next finds it, an hour or a week later. Nobody has to be online at the same time, nothing pings you, and there's nothing to like or count. When you come back, the house tells you what happened while you were away. And if friends are in at the same time, the house is live: you see each other walk, hug, and leave things as it happens.

## What "good" means here

A good version of this house:

1. **Works asynchronously.** One person can come by, leave something and go, without waiting for anyone.
2. **Remembers people.** What you leave is still there tomorrow, after a refresh, a restart or a redeploy.
3. **Makes coming back worth it.** When you return, the house tells you who has been by and what's new since your last visit.
4. **Feels like a place, not a feed.** It has rooms, a wall and a kitchen table, not a timeline. There are no likes, follower counts, notifications or infinite scroll.
5. **Is built for exactly five.** The five of us are the whole cast, and the house assumes you already know each other.
6. **Keeps interactions few and meaningful.** If something doesn't make five people feel closer, it doesn't go in.
7. **Gets more alive when friends overlap.** Being there at the same time should feel special, but it's never required.

## The house

The house is a cutaway seen from above, like a game, illustrated in Google Stitch. Amirdhavarshini's room is the big one in the corner, with the biggest bed and a round mat and cushions on the floor, because that's where everyone hangs out. The kitchen and the living room are in the middle, the garden is out the back, and Rithika's, Rithanya's, Aswathy's and Neha's rooms run along the front.

Each of us is a little sticker in the house, and nobody moves unless they're the one moving. You walk yourself around: with the arrow keys or WASD on a laptop, or on a phone by holding either bottom corner of the screen and sliding your thumb, like a game, in a room as much as in the whole house (on an iPad, the bottom corners of the house). The stick comes up wherever your thumb lands, so there's nothing to aim for, and the buttons stay out of those corners. A quick tap in a corner still taps whatever's under it. Walk into a room and a button offers to take you in (Enter does it on a keyboard), and the house zooms in on it. You can also just tap a room, and you walk there and go in. When you're done in a room, walk out of it and the house zooms back out, with you where you stepped out.

Whatever you walk up to that you can use lights up gold: a bed, a desk, the cushions on Amirdhavarshini's mat, Rithanya's mirror, Aswathy's yoga mat, the sofas, the wall of notes, the stove, the garden patches, a birthday girl's balloons, Shinzo. Walk up to a friend who's here and you can hug her. Buttons for what you can do there appear above the Go in button. Sitting on Amirdhavarshini's mat with a friend, the button deals UNO. On a keyboard, Enter or E does the first one (ahead of going in), and the number keys do the rest.

Everything you can do has a little emoji on its button: 😴 sleep, 💧 water, 🌱 plant, 🐾 pet Shinzo, 🦴 give him a treat, 🧹 tidy, 🍜 kettle Maggi, 🥒 face mask, 😋 eat, 🤗 hug, 🎂 wish a happy birthday, 🃏 UNO, and so on. The emoji pops up over the thing as you do it. Once it's done, something pops up where you did it: drops fall on the garden, a sprout comes up, hearts float over Shinzo, sparkles show where the clutter was, z's drift over you in bed.

The house keeps where you stopped. Reload, or come back next week, and you're standing there, with Shinzo beside you if he's with you.

Friends stand still where they last were, so you can find a friend in the kitchen, or on the sofa where she sat down. Anyone who's been here in the last ten minutes breathes a little and glows underfoot because she's here. During her sleep hours, a friend who isn't here is tucked up in her own bed.

The house is live. Nothing needs a refresh: whatever a friend leaves, wherever she sits down, whoever she hugs shows up on your screen as it happens, and a friend who's in at the same time walks around in front of you. Typing something yourself is never interrupted.

Each bedroom is someone's own:

- **Neha's** has a pink tablecloth, a blue checked blanket and the board games. Neha's the first one in bed, at 10.
- **Rithanya's** has UPSC books and a box of KitKats on the study table, a basket of more KitKats, a mirror, and face mask powder for everyone. She loves movies, and she sleeps in too.
- **Aswathy's** has a yoga mat for meditating, a desk with two chairs (one to work on, one for her clothes), and bananas that should have been thrown out a while ago. Tidying doesn't touch the bananas.
- **Rithika's** has shelves of little bottles and jars, a pink bed and a desk.
- **Amirdhavarshini's** has the hangout mat, with a cushion for each of us around it, and the kettle we made Maggi in back in the hostel.

Everyone has a laptop.

On a laptop, the house sits beside "while you were away" and everyone's clocks. On a phone, the house fills the screen and you pan around it, with tabs at the bottom for Home, Updates, Movies and Everyone.

Your sticker sits in the corner of the top bar ("Not Rithanya?", or whoever you are), and tapping it takes you back to the door. On the Everyone page you can also come in as someone else directly.

## Every room keeps its owner's time

A bedroom runs on its owner's clock. If it's late afternoon in Canberra and 3 am in New Jersey, Rithika's room is in evening light and Neha's is dark, with Neha asleep in bed and a lamp on in Rithika's, whoever is looking. Each room's name tag shows its owner's time. Only bedrooms ever dim: the living room, kitchen, garden and the halls between are always in daylight, whatever the time, and after dark the fire's lit too.

Characters sleep at night where they live: Neha from 10 pm to 8 am, Rithanya from 11:30 pm to 9:30 am, and everyone else from 11 pm to 7 am. Anyone who's been in the house in the last ten minutes is shown awake and "home now", so being there at the same time shows.

## What you can do

- **Leave something on a friend's desk.** Write a note, add something with it (masala chai, filter coffee, dosa, biryani, Maggi, cake, a mango, a flower, chocolate or a KitKat from Rithanya's box), and it waits on their desk. Only they can read the note; anyone who walks in sees that something's been left. A friend can write back to a note on her own desk: her answer goes on your desk, says which note it's answering, and only you can read it.
- **Write on the living room wall.** Something funny from today, a good-luck wish, anything. Tick "big news" and it stays pinned at the top. The wall starts fresh on the 1st of every month (by Canberra's clock, so everyone sees the same wall), and each month before goes into the memory box in its own envelope, so nothing ever disappears. Friends can stick a sticker on any note (❤️ 😂 🥹 🎉 🤗 😮 🙏 👀), one each, with their name on it, so whoever wrote it knows they read it and how they felt. Pick another to swap yours, or the same one to peel it off. Stickers are never counted, and only the writer hears about them.
- **Pin a photo on the memory wall.** Something remind you of everyone? Take a photo of it and pin it on the living room wall, with a line about it (it starts as "this reminds me of you guys"). It goes up like a polaroid, and friends can stick a sticker on it like any note. Only friends in the house can see it.
- **Cook for everyone, and eat what's out.** Leave a dish out on the kitchen counter. It stays out for three days, or until someone eats it: then the plate's gone from the counter for everyone, and the cook hears who ate it.
- **Save a plate for a friend who's away.** Cook something and save it for one friend. It sits on the counter covered, with her name on a tag, for however long she's away. Only she can eat it, she finds it first when she comes into the kitchen, and whoever saved it hears she found it.
- **Tidy someone's room.** Rooms get a little lived-in after a couple of days, and messier after five. Anyone can tidy any room, and the owner finds out who did.
- **Look after the garden.** Everyone has a patch to plant in. Plants sprout, grow and bloom over three days. They look thirsty after two days without water, but they never die, and anyone can water the lot.
- **Sleep or sit down anywhere.** Anyone can sleep in anyone's bed, sit on her cushion on Amirdhavarshini's mat, sit on the sofa, or meditate on Aswathy's yoga mat. Friends who come by find you there, and the bed's owner hears who slept in it. You get up when you walk, or after two hours.
- **Shinzo has his own day.** He gets up and wanders the house on his own: the garden, the kitchen, the sofas, everyone's rooms, stopping for a sniff on the way. Every so often he naps, on his blanket under the tree or curled up in someone's room, and only then is he lying down. Everyone sees him in the same place at the same moment.
- **Pet Shinzo, or give him a treat.** Walk up to him wherever he's got to. He wakes if he was napping, comes over, and follows a step behind you for half an hour, facing the way he's going. Then he goes back to his own day.
- **Hug.** Walk up to friends who are here right now and hug one of them, or pull everyone close into a group hug, up to all five of you. You stay huddled together, hearts floating over you, until someone walks off, and whoever you hugged hears about it.
- **Birthdays.** Rithanya's is in January, Amirdhavarshini's in May, Neha's in June, Rithika's in October and Aswathy's in November. All through a friend's birthday month (by her own clock) her room has bunting and balloons, and she wears a crown wherever she is. Walk up to the balloons to wish her a happy birthday: you get a party hat for a couple of hours, and she finds out who wished her. On the day itself there's cake on her desk.
- **UNO on the mat.** Sit on Amirdhavarshini's mat with friends and deal UNO: seven cards each, up to five of you. Everyone gets twenty seconds a turn, counted down on the table. Whoever lets her time run out, or leaves the house, is out of the game, and the rest play on without waiting; the last one left wins. Updates says who dealt and who won.
- **Do a face mask in Rithanya's room.** She always has the powder. Your sticker wears a green mask with cucumber slices for two hours, so a friend who comes by catches you in it, and Rithanya hears whose face used her powder.
- **Make kettle Maggi in Amirdhavarshini's room.** Like in the hostel. The kettle and two bowls sit out on her mat, steaming, for the night (twelve hours), with a note if you leave one.
- **Movie time.** Rithanya loves movies, so the Movies tab is hers. The time for all of us comes first, to plan a movie night on the living room sofas; then suggest a movie and say why, or tick off the ones you've watched. A new plan replaces the old one. Rithanya's suggestions are marked as her picks.
- **A time for all of us.** On the Everyone tab (and on Movies), every friend's clock is there at once, one row each, starting from now. Above them is a strip of everyone's day and night: drag it with a finger or the mouse (or swipe, or use the arrow keys) and every clock's hands, its AM or PM and its time follow as you go, with each friend's time beside the needle so a phone shows all five at once. Or change any one row and the rest follow, so if Neha's staying up late or you're up early, start from that row. Each row says if it falls while that friend is usually asleep. Use it just to see what time it'll be for everyone, or plan a video call (with what it's about, if you like) or a movie night from it. Everyone sees the plan in their own time, and Back to now puts the clocks back.
- **Look out at the whole house.** In any room, even lying in bed, press Esc (or See the whole house) and the view zooms out to the whole house, so you can watch who's moving about without getting up. Esc again goes back into the room.

When you come home, **While you were away** lists what's new since your last visit, starting with what was left for you: who tidied your room, what's on your desk, who wrote on the wall, what's in the kitchen. Rooms with something new for you twinkle in the house too. A visit is a stretch of page loads with no 30-minute gap, so the *new* marks survive a refresh and reset the next time you come back. The Updates tab also keeps what happened lately, so there's always something to read. After the wall starts fresh, the first time you come back you get a recap of last month's wall, every note and photo with your name, and what's new to you marked. It waits on Updates (and on a laptop's Home) however long you're away, until you put it in the memory box.

## A house for your own friends

This house is for the five of us, but anyone can make one for their own friends at `/make` (there's a link at the door), for two to seven of you. Tell it each friend's name, where she lives and a few things about her, pick what's in her room from a set of pictures (a guitar, fairy lights, too many plants, a cat bed and more), and add the memories you'd pin on the living room wall. At the end it asks for something you all did together and the inside joke or quote from it, and frames that, word for word, at the house's door and on the memory wall in the living room, with a little frame on the living room wall in the picture. The name you give the house is on its door and at the top of every page. It builds a house of your own at its own private address: our picture for five, or a house drawn for your number, with a room each named for your friends, their things pictured in their rooms, your memories on the wall, and everyone's own clock. When Claude can be reached it writes each room's description from what you said; otherwise the house is built straight from your words. Everything else works the same in your house: walking, hugs, UNO (at the coffee table, if you aren't five), the wall, Shinzo. Our five's own things (the KitKats, the face masks, the kettle) stay in our house.

It's a prototype: five friends only for now, because more friends means more rooms, and more rooms means new art.

## What isn't here yet, on purpose

Photos, drawings, notifications, and editing or deleting things you left. Each one has to show it makes five people feel closer first. Being in at the same time is a bonus, never a need: you see friends walk and what they do as it happens, but everything waits for whoever comes by later. UNO, with its twenty-second turns, is the one thing that needs you there.

Only one person can be each of us at a time. A friend's window at the door stays shut while someone's in the house as her, and opens again two minutes after they leave, even for her own second device. Anyone with the link can still pick a window that's free. There's no password on purpose for now, so the course's markers can walk in as any of us: a desk note is private from the other friends in the house, but not from someone who comes in as you. A shared front-door key is the likely next step before the real five move in.

## On your phone

The house can go on a phone's home screen like an app, with the living room's fire as its icon. On an iPhone, open it in Safari, tap Share, then Add to Home Screen. On Android, open it in Chrome and choose Add to Home screen (or Install app) from the menu. It opens full-screen, without the browser around it. A made house goes on the home screen under its own name and opens straight to its own door.

There's a widget too, like Locket. Home asks once on each phone, iPad or laptop: "Put Five Windows on your home screen?" Say yes and it shows the steps for whatever you're on, and tells you "It's working" as soon as your widget first looks in; say not now and it stops asking on that one. The widget shows something different each time your phone refreshes it: a friend's birthday (that day and the day before, with her picture), a note or a gift someone left on your desk with their words, a plate saved for you, this month's photos on the memory wall, your room with you in it, everyone's faces and clocks, and Shinzo. Tap it to go straight there. It's easy on the battery: one small question to the house each refresh, and every picture kept on the phone once it has it. On an iPhone or iPad it's a widget in the free Scriptable app (copy, paste, add); a Mac with macOS Sonoma or newer shows the iPhone's widget on its desktop. On Android it takes KWGT (its Pro key is a few dollars), since Android doesn't let a website be a widget on its own. The widget updates itself, so nobody pastes twice, and it never sends a notification. Each friend's widget is her own and says whose it is, so come in as yourself before saying yes. A made house gets all of this for its own friends, faces and rooms included.

## How it's built

It's kept as small as the problem: one Node process, no framework and no runtime dependencies. Every page is drawn on the server, and every action is a plain HTML form. One small script lets you walk your avatar around, and another keeps an open page up to date, but everything works without them: rooms are links.

- `src/server.ts` holds the routes, and lets only one person be each of us at a time.
- `src/live.ts` keeps a line open to every open page (server-sent events) and tells it when something changes or a friend walks; `public/live.js` then fetches the page again and swaps in what's new. It also plays your UNO cards without leaving the page.
- `src/store.ts` keeps everything people leave in one SQLite table (Node's built-in `node:sqlite`), plus when each friend was last here and where she stopped, in a file on the Fly volume at `/data`, the only storage that survives a redeploy.
- `src/house.ts` works out what the house looks like from that log: how messy a room is, how thirsty the plants are, where Shinzo's own day has taken him, who's asleep, sitting or hugging where, what's on the counter, who's in a face mask or a party hat, whether there's Maggi out, and the movie list.
- `src/uno.ts` replays each UNO game from its moves in the log.
- `src/time.ts` turns each friend's time zone into a clock, a time of day and her birthday month.
- `src/scene.ts` lays an SVG layer over the illustration: the rooms as links, each room's light, what's been left, the things you can use, Shinzo and the five of us.
- `src/art.ts` draws the small things in SVG: food, notes, clutter, plants, Shinzo on his feet and his blanket, and the bunting, balloons and crowns.
- `src/pages.ts` puts the pages together, `public/style.css` styles them, and `public/house.js` does the walking: the keys, the thumb stick, the Go in button, lighting up what you're next to, the hug button, telling the house where you are, walking Shinzo along his day, and moving friends as they walk.
- `src/people.ts` lists the five, with their cities, time zones, sleep hours and birthdays.
- `scripts/cut-art.py` cuts the Stitch exports into the house picture (with one desk in each room), Shinzo and the five stickers in `public/art/`. It runs by hand, not on the server.

The big decisions (the stack, the storage, who counts as a person, the illustrated house, the log of things, the clocks, walking yourself around, the house keeping where you stopped, the live house, one of us at a time, Shinzo's own day, UNO and its twenty-second turns, and always-lit common areas) are written up as decision records in `doc/adr/` in the repository.

Run it locally with `pnpm start` (it keeps its data in `./data`), then run `pnpm check` in another terminal to check it against the spec.
